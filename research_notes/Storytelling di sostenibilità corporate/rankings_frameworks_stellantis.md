## Q1. Which criteria do rankings and experts use, and which companies (automotive and overall) score best? What do the winners do?

### Takeaway
No ranking found scores "sustainability storytelling" for automotive specifically, and none places Stellantis.
- **Lundquist** scores two axes: substance (a concrete, structured, transparent journey) and distinctiveness (a credible, authentic story).
- **Comprend/Lundquist Webranking** scores transparency against stakeholder expectations. Italian utilities and energy companies (Poste, Terna, Snam, Eni) lead.
- **Expert perception (GlobeScan/ERM):** Patagonia, IKEA, Unilever and Natura &Co lead.
- **Automotive:** the only recent head-to-head found is an AI-assisted "Sustainability Reporting World Cup" (2026). BMW won it, ahead of Mercedes-Benz (semi-final) and Ford (final). Ford was judged better at storytelling and business integration, BMW better at disclosure maturity.

### Inferences
- The rankings converge on a common set of criteria. They fall into four groups:
  - **Substance and proof:** structured journey, KPIs, targets with progress.
  - **Strategy integration:** link to business value (SVP, "business sense"), KPI tied to remuneration.
  - **Distinctiveness and credibility:** an authentic story with its own voice, visuals, stakeholder voices.
  - **Transparency and accessibility:** stakeholder-led navigation; social as well as environmental topics.
- The VW case shows the "single organising idea" pattern well: one named strategy (regenerate+), four dimensions, one headline KPI.
- Storytelling-quality rankings are mostly Italy- and Nordic-centred (Lundquist, Comprend). Automakers are rarely singled out, so the benchmark will need its own scoring grid. That grid could borrow Lundquist's substance × distinctiveness axes and Comprend's stakeholder criteria.

## Q2. What are the most cited principles and pitfalls in 2024-2026?

### Takeaway
The dominant 2024-2026 themes are:
- **Close the say-do gap.** Be candid about status, targets and plan; evidence of effort beats silence.
- **Tie sustainability to business value** rather than moral value.
- **Use CSRD as a data backbone,** while recognising it creates a need for more distinctive, simpler narratives.
- **Avoid both greenwashing and greenhushing.**

From 27 Sept 2026, the EU EmpCo directive changes the risk calculus. It bans generic environmental claims without proof and net zero / neutrality claims based on offsets. It also requires future-performance claims (such as "net zero by 2050") to carry a public implementation plan that is independently verified.

### Cited Findings
**Say-do gap / progress over perfection**
- Marsh defines the say-do gap as external messaging misaligned with business realities. It advises being candid about current status, targets and plan, and warns that waiting for perfection creates an information vacuum and more scrutiny. — [Marsh](https://www.marsh.com/at/en/services/risk-advisory/insights/avoiding-the-say-do-gap-in-esg-communications.html)
- Bain, "Visionary CEO's Guide to Sustainability 2025", analysed 35,000+ statements from 150 CEOs. Rhetoric is shifting from moral value to **business value**, and Bain describes a "do-say gap": CEOs talk less while still acting. — [Bain PDF](https://www.bain.cn/pdfs/202602090304404246.pdf)
- Lundquist 2025 proposes a "Sustainability Value Proposition": show where and how sustainability creates value. — [Lundquist](https://lundquist.it/2025/05/26/sustainabilitys-next-move-from-crisis-to-ability/)

**Greenhushing**
- South Pole Net Zero Report (Jan 2024; dated):
  - 1,400+ companies surveyed across 12 countries and 14 sectors; greenhushing found in 9 of 14 sectors. — [South Pole](https://www.southpole.com/news/survey-finds-most-companies-going-quiet-on-green-goals); [Grist](https://grist.org/regulation/greenhushing-report-companies-hiding-climate-progress/)
  - 58% of those finding communication difficult intend to cut back deliberately. — [KnowESG](https://knowesg.com/reporting-standards/greenhushing-prevailing-across-sectors-says-study-29012024)
  - Lead author Nadia Kähkönen urges companies to share lessons learned. — [Grist](https://grist.org/regulation/greenhushing-report-companies-hiding-climate-progress/)
- HBR-reported study of 75 global companies: 85% maintained or expanded sustainability programmes, but only 16% publicly reaffirmed them. This is secondary; I could not access the HBR original. — [Forbes Argentina](https://www.forbesargentina.com/liderazgo/greenhushing-silencio-corporativo-sobre-sustentabilidad-riesgo-real-n95350); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91526275/the-antidote-to-greenhushing)
- MIT SMR 2025: 39% of US companies surveyed reduced or stopped public promotion of sustainability investments while maintaining them. — via [Forbes Argentina](https://www.forbesargentina.com/liderazgo/greenhushing-silencio-corporativo-sobre-sustentabilidad-riesgo-real-n95350) (secondary)
- Research on 1,512 firms (2003-2022) finds an inverted U: the most sustainability-intensive firms reduce disclosure. — [Univ. Michigan Erb Institute](https://erb.umich.edu/about/erb-news/why-some-sustainable-companies-greenhush-or-brownwash-their-own-practices)

**CSRD/ESRS and the Omnibus**
- The Omnibus was proposed in Feb 2025 and agreed and adopted in Dec 2025. Scope narrowed to companies with more than 1,000 employees and more than €450M turnover. — [Persefoni](https://www.persefoni.com/blog/what-is-csrd); [RSM](https://www.rsm.global/belgium/en/insights/sustainability-highlights-retrospection-2025)
- Verdantix: the rush to comply produced a box-ticking culture; the Omnibus pause lets double materiality become a strategic tool linked to business vision. — [Verdantix](https://www.verdantix.com/insights/blogs/the-csrd-the-omnibus-and-the-case-for-a-reset)
- WBCSD 2025: mandatory reporting improves comparability, but diverging jurisdictions widen quality gaps. New rules foster internal collaboration, value-chain focus and double materiality. — [WBCSD](https://www.wbcsd.org/resources/reporting-matters-2025-embedding-change-accelerating-impact/)
- Lundquist's "CSRD paradox": compliance reporting increases the need for more distinctive and inclusive communication. — [Lundquist PDF](https://lundquist.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/La-narrativa-strategica-ai-tempi-della-CSRD.pdf)

**EU Empowering Consumers Directive (EU) 2024/825 ("EmpCo")**
- Applies from **27 Sept 2026**, with no grandfathering. — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-empowering-consumers-directive-new-rules-on-green-claims-apply-from-27-september-2026); [Coslaw](https://coslaw.eu/directive-eu-2024-825-generic-environmental-claims-and-practices-banned-in-the-eu-from-27-september-2026/)
- Banned practices include:
  - generic environmental claims without demonstrable recognised excellent environmental performance;
  - partial claims presented as applying to the whole business;
  - offset-based "carbon neutral / net zero" claims, which may only reflect actual lifecycle and value-chain impact.
  — [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/regulations/eu-empco-directive/); [B&D Law](https://bdlaw.com/publications/eu-cracks-down-on-environmental-claims); [ISS-Corporate](https://www.iss-corporate.com/resources/blog/what-the-empowering-consumers-for-the-green-transition-directive-means-for-environmental-claims/)
- **Future-performance claims** (such as "net zero by 2050") need a detailed, realistic, public implementation plan with measurable, time-bound targets, resources, governance and monitoring. The plan must be **reviewed regularly by an independent external expert** whose findings are public. The plan can sit on the corporate website, linked from the claim (e.g. by QR code), per the European Commission's view. A missing element is "strong evidence" of a misleading practice. — [Heuking, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/statements-on-future-environmental-performance-under-the-empco-directive.html)
- Transposition: only 7 Member States met the March 2026 deadline; the Commission opened infringement procedures against 20 in May 2026. Enforcement is national, through UCPD regimes. — [Verdantix](https://www.verdantix.com/insights/blog/empco-goes-live--what-firms-need-to-know-about-the-eu's-greenwashing-crackdown)
- Sanctions are cited as fines of at least 4% of annual turnover or at least €2M. This is consultancy commentary; verify against national law. — [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/regulations/eu-empco-directive/)

### Inferences
- EmpCo covers B2C commercial practices. Corporate websites that consumers can reach, and brand-level claims ("our path to net zero"), are plausibly in scope. That pushes corporate storytelling away from adjectives ("green", "sustainable", "climate-friendly") toward **specific, quantified, verifiable** statements, each linked to a plan and to third-party verification. Legal interpretation of "corporate vs product" scope is not settled in the sources found.
- Principles cited across sources:
  - quantified proof over adjectives;
  - progress reporting, including setbacks and revised targets;
  - business-value framing;
  - stakeholder voices;
  - layered content: a narrative hub above CSRD data;
  - social as well as environmental topics.
- Pitfalls cited across sources:
  - generic claims;
  - offset-based neutrality;
  - silence or greenhushing;
  - compliance-only reporting with no narrative;
  - inconsistency between strategy cycles.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative "narrative arc" framework (purpose → transformation → proof → people → progress) attributed to a named expert; it remains a practitioner construct.
- Primary HBR greenhushing article not accessed; a 2025 South Pole update was not found.
- Official Commission guidance on corporate-level (versus product-level) scope of EmpCo was not found.

## Q3. Where does Stellantis stand, and what are its storytelling gaps?

### Takeaway
stellantis.com organises sustainability around stakeholder groups ("Engaging Our Stakeholders": customers, employees, host countries / civil society, suppliers, planet) and a carbon net zero strategy (vehicles, supply chain, industrial sites).

The **climate headline changed from net zero 2038 (Dare Forward 2030, 2022) to net zero 2050** (2025 Climate Policy Report / current site), with 2030 targets cut to -20/30% Scope 1-3. Some Stellantis pages (for example "CSR Vision") apparently still state 2038, a coherence risk.

The new FaSTLAne 2030 plan (May 2026) is framed as growth, profit and "multi-energy", with little explicit sustainability narrative in its headline communications. I found no Lundquist or Webranking position for Stellantis. Reputation indices show it well below Ferrari and declining.

### Inferences (storytelling gaps vs peers)
- **No single organising idea.** VW has "regenerate+" with four dimensions and one Decarbonization Index tied to pay. Stellantis' sustainability content is organised by stakeholder lists and technical pillars, and the old "Dare Forward" sustainability hooks (e.g. "industry champion in climate") have not visibly been replaced by a FaSTLAne-era sustainability narrative.
